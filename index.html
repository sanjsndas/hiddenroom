From the Archive: The Architecture of Everyday Life

Architecture is not only about monuments. It is about the stair you climb every morning, the window that frames a tree, the kitchen table where conversations unfold. In this ongoing series, Hidden Room Stories examines the quiet architecture of domesticity — the decisions, often invisible, that shape how we live. We speak with architects, craftspeople and residents to understand how buildings can support rather than dominate. Whether it’s a carefully placed niche for a favourite book or a corridor designed to slow your pace, the smallest moves often carry the greatest weight.

Our editors believe that good design is a form of care. It begins with listening — to the site, to the climate, to the rhythms of the people who will inhabit the space. This editorial stance guides every article we publish, from technical guides on moisture management to philosophical reflections on the meaning of home.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the best way to start an interior design project? +
Begin with a thorough brief. Before choosing colours or furniture, define how you want each room to feel and function. Collect reference images, measure your space accurately, and establish a realistic budget. Our article “The First 10 Steps of Any Interior Project” offers a detailed roadmap.
How do I choose the right lighting for a room? +
Think in layers: ambient (general light), task (for reading or cooking), accent (to highlight art or architecture), and decorative (for atmosphere). Dimmers are essential. Also consider colour temperature — 2700K for cosy living areas, 3000K–4000K for kitchens and workspaces.
Are sustainable materials more expensive? +
Not always. While some certified timber or low-VOC finishes carry a premium, many sustainable choices — like reclaimed wood, lime plaster, or locally sourced stone — can be cost-competitive. The long-term savings in durability and health often outweigh the initial outlay.
How can I make a small room feel larger? +
Prioritise natural light, use a consistent floor material throughout, choose furniture with legs to show floor area, and avoid heavy curtains. Mirrors can help, but good planning is more effective. Our guide “Small Space, Big Ideas” covers 20 practical strategies.
What’s the difference between interior design and interior architecture? +
Interior design focuses on finishes, furniture, colour and styling within existing spaces. Interior architecture involves altering the structure — moving walls, changing ceiling heights, or reconfiguring spatial volumes. Both overlap, but the latter requires a deeper understanding of building systems.
